Back in April 2020 I wrote a five part series called The DeFi Opportunity. The premise was that DeFi's first job was to rebuild the products that run the modern economy (payments, lending, investing) without rent-seeking intermediaries in the middle. Six years later, ether.fi is the closest thing I've seen to that thesis shipping at scale: a card that spends from a self-custodied account, a credit line backed by an Aave market, and yield on whatever's sitting there, in 199 countries.

Retail got there first. The part the market is still underpricing is ether.fi Business, and on November 6th Mike Silagadze made it official that they're hiring a dedicated CEO to go all in on it. I think that's massively bullish for ETHFI, and I've tried to make the case with numbers rather than vibes.

The first billion

From $5.89 to $1.1 billion in under two years.

On October 17th ether.fi Cash crossed $1B in card spend, under 23 months after the first five swipes totaled $5.89. It passed $1.1B three weeks later. Along the way: 12.6M+ purchases, 117,000+ accounts, 172,000 cards and 199 countries.

Two details on that page matter more than the headline. Purchases over $1,000 were only 1% of the count but half of the dollars, and software plus advertising was ~$87M of spend, on top of 177,984 payments to OpenAI and Anthropic. That's business spend running through consumer accounts, i.e., companies were already using ether.fi before there was a product actually built for them.

The pivot

Trading up from a $90M market to a $2.5 trillion one.

In April 2025 ether.fi published Building a $1B Revenue DeFi Protocol: a path to $1B of revenue at 30–40% margins, up to 25% of revenue for ETHFI buybacks, and a Cash business serving 6–8M retail users and 25,000 corporate clients.

Since then the mix has flipped. The card went from 17% of monthly revenue in January to 46% by July, and Mike has said the revenue run-rate is on track to grow ~38% this year (CoinInsider). The three trackers ether.fi links from its own footer show $3.8–4.3M of revenue over the last 30 days (Dune, DefiLlama, Token Terminal), so call it ~$46M annualized.

I read that as trading up. ether.fi won liquid restaking outright and is still the #3 ETH staking token in the world with 1.9M ETH and ~11% share (DefiLlama). It moved into a market tens of thousands of times bigger while it was still on top of the one it came from.

Built to last

No wood chipper. Boring on purpose.

Of everything we've tried with blockchains (NFTs, the metaverse, play-to-earn, web3 social), the use cases with durable product market fit turned out to be financial: stablecoins, RWAs, borrow/lend, trading. A few others have had their moments (memecoins, launchpads, etc.), but I share Mike's view that if you keep feeding users into the wood chipper for max extraction, you eventually run out of users.

ether.fi has done the opposite. While others lured users in with triple digit APYs, rigged points programs and obscene leverage, it kept liquidation thresholds conservative. That left borrowing power (and the interest revenue that comes with it) on the table, but it kept users out of the liquidation engine through flash crash wicks and long drawdowns. Same on security: when several Solana-based crypto neobanks were hit by an exploit in August, ether.fi was unaffected, and after October's run of exchange hacks its recap was simply "Our October was boring by comparison. And boring is good."

That's the kind of institutional grade model a business will actually sign up for. No CFO wants their company associated with the corner of crypto that profits off livestreams of people in cages, celebrity coins and rug pulls. ether.fi looks less crypto-forward on purpose, and pairs a better product (cheaper, faster, global, more options) with the things real businesses demand: 1% cashback priced to last, credit limits a board can sign off on, and up to $500K of account protection that's been announced and is on the way.

Unit economics

One business is worth many retail accounts.

ether.fi earns on six lines: card interchange, a share of the yield on balances, a cut of borrowing through its Aave market (it keeps 80% of that market's revenue), FX and payments, swaps and investing, and membership. The retail card is profitable thanks to cashback caps and breakage. The business card is profitable before either kicks in, because business cashback is 1% against a 3% headline on retail.

I'll use 4RC as the example. We've run our fund expenses on ether.fi cards since mid-2025, across five entities. The payments go to the vendors every fund has (the administrator, custody software, lawyers), almost all of them are over $1,000, and our average payment is dozens of times the network average of $80.22. Behind the cards is a treasury that stays on the platform, with a modest borrow against it that just stays drawn.

For what it's worth, the April 2025 plan put corporate LTV at $1,200–1,800 for the lifetime of the customer. By my math that's one year of card revenue alone.

The market

Priced below the fintechs it's chasing.

Mike has called neobanking "a $300 billion revenue industry." I fact checked it. As revenue neobanks earn today it's high (Revolut, the biggest in the West, did £4.5B in 2025). As the pool they're taking share from, it's conservative for business: McKinsey put bank revenue from small and mid-sized businesses at ~$850B a year back in 2019, US small business banking alone is ~$150B, and US commercial card volume was $2.23T in 2024 (Nilson). The modern players have barely scratched it, and look at what the market pays for them.

Longer term I don't think "crypto neobank" stays its own category. ether.fi should just be a neobank one day, because the banks are coming the other way: Morgan Stanley and Citi are building crypto trading and custody, Coinbase, Circle and Ripple now hold national trust bank charters, 8 of Europe's top 20 banks offer crypto under MiCA, and Ramp rolled out stablecoin accounts to every customer in July. It doesn't get self-custody, DeFi yield on idle balances, a ~4% credit line against the whole portfolio or a 25-person cost base, because those would cannibalize how it makes money today.

The product

What Business needs is mostly low hanging fruit.

I own and operate five entities on ether.fi (our funds, their management companies and our labs entities), and as the controller I reconcile all of it by hand every month. The fixes fall into two buckets, and none of them is hard. They haven't been built because the business account today is the retail product with corporate features bolted on, and nobody's full time job has been to build for a controller instead of a consumer.

Past parity, an onchain account can do things a bank never will: a payroll line drawn against the treasury inside a board policy, fund rails (capital calls into per-investor accounts, distributions in dollars or stablecoins), a treasury match on balances that stay, and a month-end close inside the AI connector, where every transaction posts by rule and the period locks with a fingerprint. That last one is how CFAgent, the fund admin and NAV agent I co-founded, already works for digital-asset funds.

And here's what none of the incumbents can copy. The whole balance sheet earns, backs a credit line and pays the bills while the company keeps custody. Settlement between two ether.fi accounts never closes. And as of November 12th a treasury can hold 1,500+ tokenized stocks, T-bills and gold in the same account and borrow against all of it. Mercury, Brex, Ramp and Slash stop at dollars and stablecoins.

The hire

A dedicated CEO turns a feature into a business.

On November 6th Mike posted that ether.fi is hiring a CEO for ether.fi Business, with their own team, control of the product and go-to-market, and the job to "make this into a billion dollar business unit."

I think this is the game changer. Business runs on a different machine than retail: channel partners, integrations, board grade controls and credit policy instead of cashback and card designs. As a feature inside retail it gets whatever engineering is left over at the end of the sprint. As its own unit it gets a roadmap and a P&L, pointed at the highest margin piece of ether.fi's own plan. Retail needs millions of users to produce what 25,000 businesses would.

The playbook is sitting there for whoever takes the job. Start with the crypto-native core (funds, protocols, treasuries and DATs), then CeFi companies, then everyone else. Sell through the people who each serve fifty companies (fund administrators, law firms, investors), with a free seat for accountants and auditors. Start beside the bank by showing a company's existing balances read-only, and earn the right to replace it.

How a payment rail of ether.fi's own can start

Every invoice is a referral

1A customer pays a vendor as they do today, with one line added: get paid instantly next time.
2The vendor opens an account to get paid in seconds and keep the 2.5–3% it loses to card fees.
3That vendor's other clients are the next prospects. Mine each bill hundreds of companies like us.
4Payments between two accounts settle account to account, with no terminal involved.
A consumer rail needs enormous scale before merchants bother to integrate. Between businesses it only needs two companies and an invoice. ether.fi gives up interchange on those payments and gets back the balances and credit every new business brings.

A good first year, by my estimate: 1,000 active businesses, $500M of balances, $300M of annualized card spend, $50M of credit drawn and zero business accounts liquidated. And the ground rules shouldn't change: the customer keeps the keys, ether.fi never earns from a liquidation, and a 40% price move doesn't wipe out a business.

The upside

Five things that aren't in my model.

Card margin. I only caught this by reading the SQL behind ether.fi's Dune dashboard: it booked 1.38% of card spend as revenue until December 2025 and has booked 1.75% since. My model uses ~1.4%. Closing that gap nearly doubles the business card margin and takes my base case 2030 value from $1.35 to about $2.04 per token.

Owning the stack. Today the card is issued by Third National, with Rain in the settlement stack. Rain raised at a $1.95B valuation in January, and ether.fi is about a quarter of the volume it settles. The April 2025 plan called for "in-house card issuing & settlement to boost margins." Owning licenses where its biggest spenders and business volume sit would add margin on every swipe plus the float on fiat balances, which is the line that makes Mercury most of its money.

The whole balance sheet moves onchain. Tokenized real-world assets are ~$39B today against forecasts in the trillions, and stablecoin payments ran at $482B through September, up 45%. Every asset that gets tokenized is one more thing a company can hold in an ether.fi account and borrow against.

Distribution it doesn't have to pay for. Binance Wallet distributes its vaults, Aave runs its credit, its card volume is most of Optimism's card settlement, Ethena powers its stablecoin, and 21Shares gives ETHFI a regulated route to European investors. Every one of those partners has a treasury and an ecosystem of companies that need a business account.

Agentic payments for businesses

The one I'm most excited about, and why ether.fi wins it

1An agent can't open a bank account. It can be handed a key to a smart contract account with a spending cap, a merchant allowlist and an expiry, enforced in code. ether.fi's accounts already are smart contracts.
2Agents pay in sub-dollar increments, around the clock and across borders. That's a stablecoin job, and ether.fi has its own stablecoin plus a Visa card behind one balance.
3Its customers are already the heaviest AI spenders around (177,984 payments to OpenAI and Anthropic), and its AI connector is live.
Gartner projects $15T of B2B spend intermediated by AI agents by 2028 and McKinsey has agentic commerce at $3–5T by 2030 (compiled here). The leading onchain agent protocol has done 138M+ transactions but only ~$19M of real volume, so the market is basically pre-launch, and Ramp, Visa, Mastercard, Stripe and Coinbase are all lining up for it.
